Dollar-funding strain is on the calendar, not yet in the overnight price

Seiche’s board reads 45.2, STRAIN, because dated Treasury settlements meet reserves below a modelled demand-curve kink. But SOFR remains below IORB and the Standing Repo Facility is unused: the structural warning has not become a cash-price break.

The dollar-funding warning is structural and dated, not yet an observed shortage in overnight cash. Seiche’s board reads 45.2, in STRAIN, led by a saturated reserve-path component tied to upcoming settlement dates. The direct market evidence points the other way: SOFR was 0.9 basis points below IORB, and Standing Repo Facility take-up was $0.00B.

That distinction is the story. Treasury settlement dates may reduce the reserve cushion, but a calendar drain is not itself proof that lenders are demanding a scarcity premium. The question is whether those dates turn a modelled capacity constraint into an observable funding-price or facility-use response.

The mechanism or historical setup

The board’s largest contribution comes from the dated reserve path: 11.0 points from a component scoring 100.0. Its premise is conditional: reserves sit below Seiche’s fitted demand-curve kink, while Treasury settlements create dates on which the system must absorb additional cash movements.

The scheduled settlements include $114B on 2026-08-31 and $262B on 2026-09-01. Seiche’s reserve-path scenarios put worst-case reserves at $2,883.9B and $2,835.2B on those dates. These are scenario outputs, not observed reserve levels and not forecasts of an overnight-rate outcome.

The proposed transmission chain is reserves, then settlement pressure, then the marginal price of overnight cash and use of the backstop. If a settlement drain reduces deployable reserve capacity enough to reach the marginal cash provider, it should appear in SOFR relative to IORB or in Standing Repo Facility use. The first link is structural; the last is market confirmation.

This is not a general call on risk assets, Treasury duration, or digital assets. It is a test of system dollar-funding capacity: whether known settlement pressure is absorbed smoothly or becomes visible in the price and use of overnight liquidity.

Quantified evidence with as-of context

Observed reserves were $2,935.287B as of 2026-08-19, down $8.8B on the week. Seiche’s balance-sheet identity attributes the movement across six legs: foreign RRP absorbed $16.0B, the balance sheet shrank $14.3B, and the residual released $10.9B. The residual still contained $8.0B, or 0.12% of assets. This is ledger evidence, not a direct observation of lenders’ willingness to deploy cash overnight.

Seiche puts its fitted reserve-demand kink at $3,702.8B and describes reserves as $768B below it as of 2026-08-19. The fit has an R-squared of 0.616. That makes the estimate a structural guide, not an observed hard threshold. The kink is a Seiche derivation using FRED, New York Fed secured-rate, and BEA GDP inputs.

Official-sector flows add context without proving immediate scarcity. Foreign officials reduced Fed custody Treasuries by $80.7B over 13 weeks while foreign RRP parking rose by $55.6B. The combined official footprint nevertheless fell $25.0B to $2,960B; Seiche calculates that 31% of the custody decline left the official sector rather than rotating within it.

The board’s divergence measure puts plumbing at the 58th percentile and market stress at the 29th percentile as of 2026-08-24. In Seiche’s reading, plumbing leads price by 29.5 percentile points. The market side remains quiet: VIX was 15.85 and the high-yield option-adjusted spread was 2.69%.

A separate alert concerns the level of the 2y Treasury constant-maturity yield, at 4.24% as of 2026-08-24, with a level z-score of 2.83. Its one-day change was 0.0. That is a level relative to its own history, not evidence of a fresh funding-market move.

The strongest counter-case

The counter-case can defeat the thesis: overnight funding may already be showing that the system has enough cash capacity to absorb the calendar.

SOFR was 3.65% as of 2026-08-24, while IORB was 3.65% as of 2026-08-26; Seiche’s direct comparison describes SOFR as 0.9 basis points below IORB as of 2026-08-19. The current reading is at or below the policy-rate anchor rather than above it. The Standing Repo Facility accepted $0.00B as of 2026-08-25. Market stress was only at the 29th percentile of its own history.

The board’s own near-term read is restrained. Its published month-end move is 2.5 basis points, with a band from -1.5 to 7.2 basis points and severity of 1 on a scale from calm to extreme. Its machine-learning event measure does not outrank the rule-based index, and the published book remains neutral.

The stronger conclusion is not merely that the warning is early. The fitted kink may be a cautious structural gauge rather than a live constraint, and the scheduled settlements may pass without a positive SOFR-to-IORB break or Standing Repo Facility demand. If that happens, the calendar-led strain thesis fails its most direct test.

A falsifiable next test

Watch the $114B settlement on 2026-08-31 and the $262B settlement on 2026-09-01. Compare the post-settlement SOFR reading with IORB and check the New York Fed’s accepted Standing Repo Facility amount.

The thesis gains support if SOFR prints above IORB after either settlement or if the facility accepts more than $0.00B. It weakens if both settlements pass with SOFR at or below IORB and accepted facility usage at $0.00B. Those conditions are observable and deliberately narrow: they test whether projected reserve pressure becomes an actual overnight-cash constraint.

Follow the pressure chain

The immediate chain is reserves, settlement dates, overnight pricing, and backstop use. A broader transmission chain could matter only if funding pressure meets constrained balance-sheet capacity or crowded exits.

Seiche’s warehouse measure shows total net positioning of $414.8B, at the 95th percentile, as of 2026-08-12; 36.0% is in the long end. That does not establish forced selling. It identifies a backdrop in which a funding impulse could have greater consequences if holders needed the same adjustment.

Offshore-dollar context remains quiet. USDT’s peg deviation was -1.8 basis points as of 2026-08-26, while total stablecoin supply was $309.0B and its 30-day change was 0.34%. These are context signals, not evidence that proves or disproves strain in the US secured-funding market.

The research boundary matters. Seiche covers system dollar-funding capacity. LiquiLens covers institution and lender balance-sheet risk. Undertow covers market liquidity, crowding, and exit capacity. These channels can interact, but they are not interchangeable and should not be compressed into a single event claim.
