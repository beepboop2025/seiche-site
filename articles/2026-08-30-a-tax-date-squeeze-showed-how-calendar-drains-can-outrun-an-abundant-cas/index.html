A tax-date squeeze showed how calendar drains can outrun an abundant-cash tape

The September 2025 episode is a historical replay, not breaking news and not a forecast. Its lesson is that dated cash removal can test dealer capacity before a broader funding signal appears.

Today’s tape did not clear the full-story gate. The desk’s newsworthiness score is 25, below the 55 required for a full story, because there was no fresh measured change. This is a historical replay, not breaking news and not a forecast.

The selected episode, the September 2025 tax-date squeeze, makes a narrow proposition testable. A tax-date drain and Treasury settlement pressure can remove deployable cash when dealer intermediation is needed. If capacity is insufficient, the first confirmation should appear in overnight funding prices and, potentially, use of the standing facility—not necessarily in broad risk markets. Similarity does not mean recurrence.

The mechanism or historical setup

The September 2025 squeeze combined a tax-date drain with Treasury settlement pressure. The desk guide describes the sequence as dated cash removal, limited dealer capacity, then a visible funding response. SOFR rose 18bp over EFFR. The episode is dated 2025-09-15; the analog ends on 2025-09-12. The engine records an event within five business days and a maximum five-business-day move of 9.0bp.

The lesson is not that a calendar date creates a crisis. A dated drain becomes consequential only if the cash and balance-sheet capacity needed to absorb it are unavailable at the relevant point in the system. The historical episode therefore supplies a disciplined sequence to observe: identify the scheduled drain, watch whether secured funding shifts relative to its policy anchor, and then examine whether the standing backstop is used.

The desk guide calls the episode smaller than September 2019, but treats it as a cleaner modern test of the same mechanism. That is the appropriate use of the replay: a calendar event is a pre-registered test, not an alarm.

Quantified evidence with as-of context

The current board is a Seiche derivation, not an observed market price. It reads 44.9 out of 100, in EROSION. The dated reserve path contributes 11.0 points. The Tell is +38.1 as of 2026-08-27: plumbing is at the 58th percentile and market pricing at the 20th percentile, hence “plumbing leads price.”

The structural case begins with the ledger. As of 2026-08-26, reserves were $2,924.936B, down $10.4B on the week. The foreign RRP pool released $17.9B, the residual absorbed $14.9B, and the Federal Reserve balance sheet shrank $14.8B. Six legs sum to the reserve change to the dollar, while $22.9B of the balance sheet, or 0.34% of assets, remained in an unnamed residual. That residual limits precision; it does not establish a cause.

A fitted reserve-demand curve places current reserves below an estimated kink, with an R-squared of 0.617. This is model structure rather than an observed threshold. It conflicts with the live price signal: SOFR was 3.64% as of 2026-08-27 and IORB was 3.65% as of 2026-08-31. The editorial read describes SOFR as 0.9bp below IORB—an abundance signal.

The calendar supplies the tests. The 2026-08-31 month-end pressure date has forecast reserves of $2,923.1B and a worst-case reserve level of $2,893.5B. On 2026-09-03, a $308.0B auction settlement coincides with forecast reserves of $2,936.4B and a worst-case level of $2,883.6B. The path also marks 2026-09-15, with a $2,844.8B worst case, and 2026-09-16, with a $2,817.2B worst case. These are Seiche scenarios, not realized reserve levels.

The strongest counter-case

The counter-case can defeat the thesis: current funding prices do not corroborate scarcity. SOFR is below IORB. Standing Repo Facility accepted volume was $0.00B as of 2026-08-28, so the backstop was not being tested in size. Market stress was at the 20th percentile of its own history as of 2026-08-27.

A fitted kink can be an unreliable guide to the marginal price of cash. Reserves can sit below an estimated kink while remaining adequately distributed among institutions that need them. A scheduled settlement can be absorbed without a persistent secured-rate dislocation or facility use. The published month-end forecast is the naive 2.2bp, inside a -1.8bp to 6.9bp band, with severity 1 on the stated calm-to-extreme scale. The 10.5bp model estimate was not the published forecast.

The analog does not resolve this dispute. Its similarity is 0.61, and the desk warns that the engine fields are construction point-in-time comparisons, not publication-vintage backtests or forecasts. The broader analog work says analogs do not beat climatology on its sample. Historical comparison can define the test; it cannot determine the result beforehand.

A falsifiable next test

The observable test begins at month-end on 2026-08-31 and continues around the $308.0B settlement on 2026-09-03. The historical mechanism gains support only if scheduled pressure is followed by a visible secured-funding response relative to IORB, moving away from the present SOFR-below-IORB configuration, and by meaningful Standing Repo Facility use.

The concern is weakened if SOFR remains below IORB, facility take-up remains negligible, and these calendar dates pass without a material funding response. The 2026-09-15 tax date and 2026-09-16 policy date provide additional dated observations. They do not permit the thesis to be retrofitted after the fact.

Follow the pressure chain

Start with the reserve ledger and the dated reserve path. Follow the scheduled drain through Treasury settlement and tax dates. Then observe the marginal price of secured cash against IORB and use of the standing facility. Only then assess whether a funding response has broadened.

For this brief, the chain stops at pricing. Structural and calendar signals lead, while overnight cash still indicates abundance. That is why confidence is guarded: modelled or slow-moving structure has not received broad current confirmation.
