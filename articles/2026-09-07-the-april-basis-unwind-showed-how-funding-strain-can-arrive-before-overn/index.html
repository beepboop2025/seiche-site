The April basis unwind showed how funding strain can arrive before overnight cash looks scarce

A historical replay of the April 2025 tariff-shock basis unwind: volatility, leveraged positions and dealer intermediation can form a pressure chain before overnight cash signals scarcity. Today’s tape did not clear the full-story gate.

Today’s tape did not clear the full-story gate. Seiche’s newsworthiness score is 25, below the 55 required for a full story, because there is no fresh measured change. This is therefore a historical replay, not breaking news and not a forecast.

The lesson from the April 2025 tariff-shock basis unwind is narrower and more useful: dollar-funding capacity can tighten through the ability of dealers and leveraged holders to finance, hedge and exit correlated positions—not simply through the aggregate quantity of reserves. Overnight cash can still look abundant while that marginal capacity comes under strain.

The mechanism or historical setup

The desk describes the April 2025 episode as a tariff-shock basis unwind. A policy shock changed risk and volatility faster than leveraged relative-value books could adjust. Treasury selling, basis pressure and dealer intermediation became a single transmission chain rather than separate market stories.

The mechanism runs from shock to volatility, then to leveraged-position adjustment. If many holders seek the same exit or hedge, flows concentrate in instruments that dealers must intermediate. As dealers absorb inventory and volatility, balance-sheet capacity becomes more valuable and potentially more selective. The relevant funding question is not only whether cash exists in aggregate. It is whether balance-sheet-intensive trades can remain funded while exits crowd together.

That does not mean a large position is automatically dangerous, or that every reserve drain becomes a market event. The historical lesson is conditional: strain can emerge when many holders require the same exit while dealers absorb the same volatility shock. An orderly overnight benchmark can coexist with impaired marginal capacity to warehouse risk.

The strongest counter-case

The counter-case is strong enough to defeat a present-tense scarcity claim. SOFR was 0.8bp below IORB as of September 2: a current abundance signal rather than a scarcity print. The Standing Repo Facility accepted $0.00B as of September 4, so the backstop was not being tested in size. Market stress was at only the 18th percentile of its own history as of September 3.

This evidence matters more for current conditions than an estimated reserve kink or a scheduled settlement. Structural and calendar signals can identify where pressure may emerge, but they do not demonstrate that funding capacity is already failing. Plumbing sits at the 65th percentile of its own history while market pricing is at the 18th, a 46.7 percentile-point gap. That is a reason to watch, not to declare an event.

The pooled five-business-day event read is 6.4%, but its probability levels do not beat climatology and are intended for ranking and alerting rather than literal odds. The analog framework also does not beat climatology on its sample. The disciplined conclusion is that overnight pricing and zero facility use presently outweigh the structural warning.

A falsifiable next test

The test is dated and observable. Watch the September 10 and September 15 settlements, the September 16 calendar-pressure date, and quarter-end on September 30. The published quarter-end forecast is 5.2bp, with a 1.3bp to 9.9bp band; it is the naive forecast rather than the model forecast.

The historical mechanism gains support only if the calendar pressure is followed by observable market confirmation: SOFR is no longer below IORB, Standing Repo Facility take-up rises above $0.00B, or market stress rises above its present 18th-percentile reading. If the scheduled dates pass while SOFR remains below IORB, facility take-up remains $0.00B and market stress stays at or below the present reading, the case that capacity is tightening through this channel is weakened.

Calendar pressure without price confirmation remains a watch condition, not evidence of a funding event.

Follow the pressure chain

Read the chain in order: shock, volatility, leveraged-position adjustment, dealer intermediation, financing and exit capacity. The April replay cautions against treating Treasury cash, futures basis, repo and reserve levels as isolated dashboards.

It also cautions against turning them into one alarm. Seiche’s stance is neutral and unchanged from the prior letter. The current reading is that the calendar carries the strain call while overnight cash still says abundance.
