Auction settlements put dollar-funding capacity on watch, but spot markets have yet to confirm strain

Seiche’s board is in EROSION as a dated reserve-path warning builds into quarter-end. The question is whether settlement pressure converts a structural signal into observable stress in secured funding.

Dollar-funding capacity looks tighter in the board’s structural and calendar measures than it does in spot funding markets. The fresh change is a 44 out of 100 EROSION reading, up 1.7 against the last published letter, led by the dated reserve path. But the evidence does not yet describe a squeeze: SOFR remains below IORB, Standing Repo Facility take-up is zero, and market stress is at the 16th percentile of its own history.

That leaves a narrower, testable thesis. Treasury settlements may test capacity through the reserve-distribution and balance-sheet channel. They have not yet produced broad confirmation in the rates that would show the test is binding.

The mechanism or historical setup

The mechanism is conditional. Treasury settlement can pull cash through the Treasury General Account, requiring adjustments in reserves and intermediary balance sheets. If deployable reserves are already low relative to demand, providers of secured funding may become more selective. The observable downstream consequence would be a deterioration in secured overnight funding relative to its policy anchor.

Seiche’s fitted reserve-demand curve places reserves $689B below its estimated kink as of 2026-09-16. That kink is a Seiche derivation from FRED, New York Fed secured-rate data and BEA GDP inputs, not an observed market-clearing threshold. Its fit has an R-squared of 0.62. It is therefore a structural warning, not a hard boundary or proof that intermediaries are short of cash.

The calendar supplies the immediate forcing. The board identifies a $175B settlement on 2026-09-29, a $44B pressure date on 2026-09-30, and a $316B settlement on 2026-10-01. Its worst-case reserve paths are $2,971.5B, $2,953.2B and $2,905.5B. Those are scenario outputs, not realised reserve forecasts. The 2026-09-30 quarter-end turn has a published naive estimate of 5.2bp, with a 1.2bp to 9.9bp band; the model estimate is 10.5bp, but the published reading remains the naive estimate.

Quantified evidence with as-of context

The Seiche composite reads 44, EROSION, with full coverage. The dated reserve-path component scores 84 and contributes 9.2 points, its largest contribution. The reserve-demand component scores 57.6 and contributes 7.5 points. Plumbing is at the 67th percentile while market pricing is at the 16th percentile, a 51.7-point tell described as “plumbing leads price.” The composite is a Seiche derivation, not an observed market price.

The latest balance-sheet ledger is more mixed than a simple tightening narrative. Reserves rose $22.5B in the week through 2026-09-16 to $3,014B. The ledger attributes a $30.8B release from the foreign RRP pool, a $16.4B absorption by the unnamed residual, and a $6.3B TGA spend-down among six legs that reconcile to the reserve change. The unnamed residual was $47.7B, or 0.71% of assets, limiting clean causal interpretation.

Foreign official custody Treasuries fell $22.5B and foreign RRP cash fell $10.4B over 13 weeks through 2026-09-16, leaving an official-sector footprint of $2,927B. Those changes show smaller official pools, but do not identify the marginal funding provider or establish dollar-funding scarcity.

The dated warning is not yet matched by a broad spot-funding break. The board’s supplied comparison puts SOFR 0.7bp below IORB as of 2026-09-16, an abundance signal. Its later US basin reading, SOFR minus IORB, was -3.0bp as of 2026-09-23. The direction matters more than the structural model: neither reading is evidence of a contemporaneous broad shortage.

The strongest counter-case

The counter-case can defeat the thesis. Settlement pressure may pass through a system with sufficient usable reserves and balance-sheet capacity, producing only a routine quarter-end move rather than a funding disruption.

The direct transmission indicators support that possibility. SOFR is below IORB. Standing Repo Facility accepted volume was $0.00B as of 2026-09-24, so the official backstop is not being tested in size. Market stress was at the 16th percentile as of 2026-09-23. The board also characterises the current state as locally contracting, with perturbations near today’s state expected to damp.

The predictive evidence should not be overstated. The model’s event-probability levels do not beat climatology, and the published book remains neutral. A related curve is assessed as beating climatology out of sample, but that does not supersede the absence of confirmation in SOFR or SRF usage. The stronger interpretation is that the calendar creates an opportunity to observe whether a structural warning transmits into prices; it does not establish that transmission in advance.

A falsifiable next test

The test is the settlement window spanning 2026-09-29 through 2026-10-01. The tightening thesis gains support only if scheduled pressure coincides with observable deterioration in SOFR relative to IORB and with SRF use or broader market-stress confirmation.

The thesis fails if the dates pass while SOFR remains below IORB, SRF take-up remains negligible, and the quarter-end move stays near the published 5.2bp estimate or inside its stated band. That outcome would show that reserve-path pressure did not translate into a binding funding-capacity constraint. It would not disprove the reserve model in every setting, but it would defeat the present claim that this calendar window is tightening dollar funding in a market-relevant way.

The boundaries matter. An aggregate funding rate can remain calm while a particular lender is impaired. Equally, a structural reserve warning can fade before reaching institutions or market exit capacity. Offshore-dollar context is not currently a break signal: the USDT peg deviation was -3.2bp as of 2026-09-24, while total stablecoin supply was $311.0B and its 30-day change was 0.8%. Those are context readings, not proof of insulation from dollar-plumbing stress.

Research boundary

This analysis addresses system dollar-funding capacity only. It does not diagnose institution or lender solvency, nor does it treat market-liquidity or exit-capacity conditions as inputs to the Seiche composite. Related Palimpsest readings are context-only and never enter the composite or a model feature.
