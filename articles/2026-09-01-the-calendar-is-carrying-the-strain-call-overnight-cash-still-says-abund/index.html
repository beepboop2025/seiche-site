The calendar is carrying the strain call; overnight cash still says abundance

Seiche’s board reads STRAIN, led by the dated reserve path. But SOFR remains below IORB on the matched comparison, Standing Repo Facility use is minimal, and market stress is subdued: the calendar has not yet become a funding event.

Dollar-funding capacity is tightening as a dated reserve-and-intermediation risk, not yet as an observed shortage in overnight cash. Seiche’s composite reads 45.7, STRAIN, up 0.6 against the last published letter. Its largest contribution comes from the saturated dated reserve path, while the price of overnight cash and use of the Federal Reserve’s repo backstop still point to abundance.

That divergence is the story. Calendar demands can test where reserves and balance-sheet capacity sit before they show up in a broad secured-funding dislocation. The evidence supports a watch for that mechanism, not a declaration that scarcity has arrived.

The mechanism or historical setup

Aggregate reserves do not by themselves establish deployable funding capacity. The relevant mechanism is a pressure chain: settlement and payment dates draw on cash; dealers and lenders must intermediate collateral and funding; secured overnight rates reveal whether that capacity is becoming scarce.

The desk’s historical guide is the September 2019 repo spike. Corporate tax payments and Treasury settlement needs converged with less immediately deployable cash than the calm aggregate picture suggested. Secured overnight rates moved before a broader risk-market crisis narrative emerged. Its lesson is narrow: a large reserve total can coexist with a local shortage of usable balance sheet.

That episode is context, not a forecast. Seiche’s historical comparison identifies it with similarity of 0.596 and a lead of 2 business days, but the comparison is construction point-in-time output, not a publication-vintage backtest or forecast.

Quantified evidence with as-of context

The observed ledger has weakened. As of 2026-08-26, reserves fell $10.4B on the week to $2,925B. The foreign RRP pool released $17.9B, the residual absorbed $14.9B, and the balance sheet shrank $14.8B. Six legs reconcile to the reserve change, though $22.9B of the balance sheet, 0.34% of assets, remains in an unnamed residual. The underlying series are available through Seiche’s data overview and series index.

A fitted reserve-demand curve places reserves $779B below its estimated kink, with R-squared of 0.62, also as of 2026-08-26. This is structural model output rather than a traded threshold. It matters because scheduled dates coincide with projected low points: Seiche’s worst-case reserve scenarios are $2,890.6B for the $137B settlement on 2026-09-03 and $2,895.3B for the $247B settlement on 2026-09-08. Those are scenarios, not observed reserve prints.

The official-sector evidence does not support a simple disappearance of foreign demand. Over 13 weeks through 2026-08-26, foreign officials cut Fed custody Treasuries by $64.4B while foreign RRP parking rose $56.4B, leaving a footprint of $2,971B. The board interprets that as a parking rotation rather than the official bid leaving.

The board’s divergence measure is material but incomplete. Plumbing is at the 60th percentile, market stress at the 29th percentile, a 31.5-percentile-point gap as of 2026-08-31. The rule-based event read is 6.0%; a separate model reading is 1.9% and explicitly does not outrank the index. The appropriate label is “plumbing leads price,” not “price confirms stress.” The full dated read is in the daily dispatch.

The strongest counter-case

The counter-case can defeat the thesis because the most direct price signal is benign. On the matched comparison as of 2026-08-26, SOFR was 0.9bp below IORB—an abundance signal, not a scarcity premium. The Standing Repo Facility accepted $0.01B as of 2026-09-01, so the backstop is not being tested in size. Market stress was only at the 29th percentile as of 2026-08-31.

This is not merely an absence of confirmation. It permits a competing explanation: the reserve-kink estimate may overstate the relevance of aggregate reserve levels, while the institutions that finance settlements retain enough balance-sheet capacity to absorb the calendar flows. The fitted curve is imperfect, the composite is a Seiche derivation rather than an observed market price, and scheduled settlements can pass without a meaningful funding response.

The current market evidence therefore says that a broad dollar-funding squeeze is not established. For the thesis to survive, the calendar signal must become visible in secured funding behavior.

A falsifiable next test

The immediate tests are observable. Treasury settlement of $137B is scheduled for 2026-09-03 and $247B for 2026-09-08. The tightening thesis gains support if these dates are accompanied by secured overnight funding moving away from its benign relationship to IORB and by Standing Repo Facility use becoming material.

It is weakened if the settlements pass while SOFR remains contained relative to IORB and Standing Repo Facility use remains minimal. That outcome would show that the dated reserve path did not translate into a broad constraint on funding capacity.

Quarter-end on 2026-09-30 is the larger published test. Seiche’s forecast is 8.0bp, with a 4.0bp to 12.6bp band. It is a scenario, not an outcome claim. The observable verdict is whether realised funding behavior confirms the calendar pressure or remains consistent with abundance.

Follow the pressure chain

Begin with the observable inputs in the data overview: reserves, Treasury cash, RRP, SOFR, IORB, and Standing Repo Facility use. Then track settlements and reserve-path scenarios in the daily dispatch.

The chain is reserve distribution, dated settlement demand, dealer and lender intermediation capacity, secured overnight pricing, and broader market stress. A break at the first stages is not equivalent to a break at the last.
