A high two-year yield meets a calendar-led dollar-funding strain call

Seiche’s board reads STRAIN, but the decisive pressure is scheduled Treasury settlement against reserves below an estimated kink—not broad confirmation from overnight cash pricing.

The material new board change is the latest two-year Treasury constant-maturity yield print: 4.19%, a 2.61-sigma level against its own history as of 2026-08-20. It raises a narrower question than the yield itself: is dollar-funding capacity tightening before the overnight cash market confirms it?

Seiche’s answer is guarded. Its composite reads 45.1, or STRAIN, but the dated reserve path—not current overnight pricing—carries the call. Scheduled Treasury settlements coincide with reserves that the fitted framework places below its estimated kink. That is a testable capacity concern, not evidence that present scarcity has already taken hold.

The mechanism or historical setup

The proposed transmission path is dated settlement pressure meeting a lower reserve starting point. Seiche identifies $266.0B of Treasury settlement on 2026-08-27 and $183.0B on 2026-08-31. The relevant proposition is conditional: if those settlements meet limited capacity to absorb them, secured overnight funding could become more sensitive to the drain.

The fitted reserve-demand framework places reserves $768B below its estimated kink, with an R-squared of 0.62. That fit is context, not a precise threshold. Its implication is simply that the reserve base may have less room to absorb scheduled pressure before rates react.

The balance-sheet ledger provides the immediate backdrop. Reserves fell $8.8B in the week to 2026-08-19, to $2,935B. The foreign reverse-repo pool absorbed $16.0B and the balance sheet shrank $14.3B, while the residual released $10.9B. The six legs sum to the reserve change, though $8.0B of the balance-sheet move remains in the unnamed residual.

Foreign officials reduced Fed custody Treasuries by $80.7B over 13 weeks while foreign reverse-repo parking rose by $55.6B. The combined official footprint still fell by $25.0B to $2,960B. Seiche calculates that 31% of the custody decline left the official sector rather than rotating within it. That is a changing official-sector footprint, not proof of a funding event.

Quantified evidence with as-of context

The 45.1 reading is a Seiche derivation, not an observed market price. Its largest contribution is the dated reserve path: the weather component is saturated at 100.0 and contributes 11.0 points. The reserve-kink component contributes 7.4 points. The decomposition matters because it explains why the regime reading is firmer than the live overnight tape.

For 2026-08-27, Seiche forecasts reserves of $2,947.9B and a worst-case reading of $2,894.8B alongside the $266.0B settlement. For 2026-08-31, the comparable figures are $2,946.2B and $2,879.7B alongside $183.0B of settlement. These are modelled reserve-path scenarios, not observed reserve prints.

The board also flags pressure dates on 2026-09-15, 2026-09-16 and 2026-09-30, with worst-case reserve readings of $2,842.2B, $2,814.7B and $2,779.5B. The calendar supplies dates for scrutiny; it does not establish that a disruption will occur.

The divergence between plumbing and price is the central contested signal. Seiche places plumbing at the 58th percentile of its own history and market stress at the 35th percentile, a 23.3 percentile-point gap described as “plumbing leads price.” That supports a watch for capacity pressure ahead of market pricing, but not a declaration of an active dollar-funding shortage.

A mid-month funding pop of 4.0 basis points is classified by Seiche as genuine scarcity. It should not dominate the evidence, however. For month-end, the forecast actually published is a naive 2.2-basis-point estimate, with a band of -1.8 to 6.9 basis points and severity of 1 on the stated calm-to-extreme scale. The model estimate of 9.6 basis points was not the published forecast.

The strongest counter-case

The counter-case can defeat the thesis: overnight money still signals abundance. SOFR was 3.63% as of 2026-08-20, while IORB was 3.65% as of 2026-08-24. Seiche characterises the 0.9-basis-point gap below IORB as an abundance signal, not a scarcity print. EFFR was also 3.63% as of 2026-08-20.

The backstop is not being tested in size. Standing Repo Facility accepted take-up was $0.0B on 2026-08-21. Broader stress also remained at the 35th percentile of its own history, with the VIX at 16.01 and the high-yield option-adjusted spread at 2.75% as of 2026-08-20.

Those facts directly challenge the mechanism. If reserve scarcity is economically binding, secured funding should show it through the policy anchor or through meaningful recourse to the backstop. So far, it has not done so broadly. Seiche’s pooled five-business-day event read is 5.9%, while its underlying probability output does not outrank the rule-based index and is not to be read as literal odds.

A falsifiable next test

The first clean test is the $266.0B settlement on 2026-08-27. The strain thesis gains support only if that scheduled pressure is followed by observable deterioration in secured funding relative to IORB, meaningful Standing Repo Facility use, or a broader rise in market-stress measures.

The thesis weakens materially if SOFR remains below IORB and the facility remains unused through the settlement window despite the projected reserve pressure. A benign 2026-08-31 month-end would provide a second disconfirmation, particularly against the published 2.2-basis-point, severity-1 path.

The calendar therefore supplies a pre-registered test rather than a prediction. The date identifies the proposed pressure point; the funding tape determines whether the mechanism is operating.

Follow the pressure chain

Start with the scheduled Treasury settlement and the reserve path relative to the fitted kink. Then observe SOFR relative to IORB, Standing Repo Facility take-up, and whether broader market stress rises toward the plumbing signal.

The chain can fail at any link. The fitted kink may not identify a binding level; reserves may prove sufficient; or the settlement may pass without disorder in secured funding. Conversely, deterioration in secured funding without a comparable rise in broad market stress would support the narrower claim that plumbing is leading price.

Sources, method, and limits

Underlying public inputs and Seiche outputs are available through the data overview, methodology, series index, daily dispatch, and dispatch archive. The composite is a deterministic synthesis of published point-in-time engines. Its confidence measure describes agreement across independent signals, not the probability of a market outcome.

The limits are central. The composite is not an observed market price. Source publication time is not yet collected uniformly, so event, desk-knowledge and publication clocks remain separate. The reserve-kink estimate is fitted, and the dated reserve paths are scenarios. Historical comparisons are context, not forecasts.
