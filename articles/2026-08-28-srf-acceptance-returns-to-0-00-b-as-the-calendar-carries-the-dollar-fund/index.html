SRF acceptance returns to 0.00 $B as the calendar carries the dollar-funding warning

Seiche’s board reads 45, EROSION, led by a dated reserve path rather than an overnight cash squeeze. SOFR remains below IORB, SRF acceptance is 0.00 $B, and market stress is low: calendar pressure has not yet become an observed funding shortage.

The material new board change is in the Federal Reserve backstop, but it is not a large draw: Standing Repo Facility accepted volume is 0.00 $B as of 2026-08-28, following a sharp one-day change on a near-zero base. The move is statistically conspicuous on the board, not evidence of a large funding flow.

The warning instead sits in Seiche’s dated reserve path. The board reads 45, or EROSION, with that path contributing 11.0 points. The proposition is conditional: scheduled drains may expose less elastic dollar-funding capacity before scarcity appears in overnight pricing. Current pricing has not confirmed it.

The mechanism or historical setup

The mechanism begins with the distinction between a reserve path and a funding event. Reserves fell $10.4B on the week to $2,925B as of 2026-08-26. Seiche’s fitted reserve-demand curve places reserves $779B below its estimated kink, although the fit has an R-squared of 0.62. That is model structure, not a hard threshold and not proof that funding is scarce.

The proposed transmission is narrow. Calendar-driven flows can absorb reserves; if private intermediation is less elastic at that point, the constraint should become visible in secured overnight pricing relative to IORB or in use of the Federal Reserve backstop. Until either occurs, the reserve-path warning remains a warning.

Seiche identifies month-end on 2026-08-31 as a pressure date and a $308B auction settlement on 2026-09-03. Its worst-case reserve paths reach $2,893.5B and $2,883.6B. The later tax date on 2026-09-15 and FOMC date on 2026-09-16 carry worst-case paths of $2,844.8B and $2,817.2B. These are scenario outputs from Seiche’s reserve path, not observed reserve prints.

The desk’s guide to the December 2025 year-end squeeze supplies the relevant historical setup: reporting incentives reduced willingness to intermediate while balance-sheet demand was seasonally high, and heavy SRF use made the constraint observable. That guide is background, not a forecast. Today’s 0.00 $B accepted volume is the opposite of such confirmation.

Quantified evidence with as-of context

The observed ledger shows a reserve decline but not a simple disappearance of liquidity. The foreign RRP pool released $17.9B, the residual absorbed $14.9B, and the Federal Reserve balance sheet shrank $14.8B in the week to 2026-08-26. Six legs sum to the reserve change, while $22.9B, or 0.34% of assets, remains in the unnamed residual.

Foreign-official data do not provide a clean stress confirmation. Foreign officials cut Fed custody Treasuries by $64.4B over 13 weeks while foreign RRP parking rose by $56.4B. Seiche describes that as a parking rotation rather than the official bid leaving; the reported footprint was $2,971B.

The board’s divergence is between plumbing and market pricing. Plumbing is at the 58th percentile and market stress at the 20th percentile, a +38 percentile-point reading that plumbing leads price. The pooled five-business-day event read is 6.1%, while a separate model estimate is 1.6%. Neither is an observed market price or a certainty claim.

The strongest counter-case

The counter-case can defeat the thesis: dollar funding is abundant now, and modeled calendar pressure has not overcome direct market evidence.

SOFR was 3.64% as of 2026-08-27, below IORB at 3.65%, a -0.9bp relationship in the editorial read. That is an abundance signal rather than a scarcity print. EFFR was 3.63% on the same date. SRF accepted volume was 0.00 $B as of 2026-08-28, so the backstop is not being tested in size. Market stress sits at the 20th percentile of its own history.

The board itself assigns only severity 1 to the next month-end turn. Its published forecast is the naive 2.2bp estimate, with a band from -1.8bp to 6.9bp. The confidence assessment is guarded because modeled or slow-moving structure leads while current market plumbing has not broadly confirmed it.

This is not a situation in which a low projected reserve level can substitute for evidence of impaired funding. If secured overnight pricing stays below IORB and the SRF remains unused through the calendar dates, the thesis fails for this window.

A falsifiable next test

The first observable test is month-end on 2026-08-31, followed by the $308B settlement on 2026-09-03. The tightening case gains support only if calendar pressure is accompanied by a sustained move in secured funding away from its current below-IORB relationship and by meaningful SRF acceptance or other direct evidence that private capacity is being tested.

The case fails for this window if SOFR remains below IORB, SRF acceptance remains near zero, and the scheduled dates pass without deterioration in the observable funding complex. A low reserve-path scenario alone does not count as confirmation.

The dates on 2026-09-15 and 2026-09-16 provide a second observation window. They are places to look, not predictions of a funding event.

Follow the pressure chain

Watch the chain in order: scheduled drains; reserve absorption; private intermediation; secured overnight pricing relative to IORB; SRF use; then broader market response.

The evidence currently reaches the first links. The ledger records lower reserves and residual absorption, and the dated path identifies calendar windows. It does not yet establish stressed secured funding, substantial backstop use, or broad market stress.
